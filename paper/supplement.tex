\begin{abstract}\raggedright\small
This supplement applies the paper's accounting framework to all 81 conflicts in the 1900--2026 dataset. For every war we compute total death ranges, civilian share ranges, combatant share ranges $q$, the share of civilian deaths that are indirect, a reproducible confidence grade, and the under fudgible relation most likely to bind if a contradiction radius analysis were performed. Only conflicts with independent demographic samples can receive a sharp contradiction radius; the rest are treated as partial identification cases.
\end{abstract}

\section{How to read the tables}
The \emph{civilian share} interval is computed conservatively as $[D_C^{lo}/(D_C^{lo}+D_M^{hi}),\;D_C^{hi}/(D_C^{hi}+D_M^{lo})]$. The $q$ interval is the corresponding combatant share. \emph{Missing figures are treated explicitly rather than as zeros}: when the sources state no ceiling for one casualty class, the ceiling is either taken from the curated per-conflict totals (with the reasoning recorded in the data file's notes) or closed mechanically by the grand-total residual---the total-death ceiling minus the other class's floor. Should a conflict identify only a one-sided bound, it is marked $\le x\%$ (combatant deaths incompletely attributed) or $\ge x\%$ (civilian deaths incompletely attributed), or \emph{n.a.} if neither class is attributable; after curation no conflict in the current dataset requires these marks. The \emph{binding relation} column is a triage label: \texttt{manpower budget} means the population/combatant-stock bound is the first place to look; \texttt{identified-deaths sample} means sex-age microdata would likely dominate; \texttt{excess-mortality survey} means direct attribution is less informative than survey-based excess deaths; \texttt{source harmonisation} marks conflicts where definitions, overlap, or missing high bounds dominate.

\paragraph{Confidence grade.} A: total-death range ratio $<2$ and civilian-share width $<15$ percentage points. B: either ratio $<4$ or width $<30$pp. C: either ratio $<8$ or width $<45$pp. D: wider than that. Conflicts with a one-sided share bound are graded on the widened (identified) interval, so an incompletely attributed class demotes the grade rather than flattering it. These grades score internal numerical precision, not moral seriousness or historical importance.

\section{Dataset summary}
The dataset contains 81 conflicts. Confidence grades: A=26, B=24, C=16, D=15, not attributable=0. Structural classes: combat-heavy=9, mixed=32, civilian-targeting=15, indirect=25.
